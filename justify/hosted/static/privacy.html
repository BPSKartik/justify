What Justify keeps, and why

Plainly, in one page. Last changed 3 October 2026.

Without an account

Justify keeps the audits you start of public repositories — they are results about public code, cached so the same commit is never scanned twice, and listed under “Recently audited”. To enforce the daily allowance it counts audits per network address per day; those counts are deleted after 60 days.

With an account

  • Your profile from GitHub or Microsoft: name, username, picture link and, if the provider shares it, email. Never a password, and never access to your repositories.
  • Your history: which audits you asked for and when, so the dashboard can show trends and what changed.
  • Your tokens and connected apps, stored only as fingerprints (SHA-256): the database cannot reveal them.
  • One cookie, to keep you signed in. No tracking, no analytics, no advertising.

Code you upload

It is unpacked on the server, audited, and deleted when the audit finishes. The result — file names, line numbers, unit names, never your code — is visible only to your account. Nothing is sent to a model or to anyone else.

Where it lives

On Microsoft Azure in Central India: the service, and a private storage container it reaches with its own managed identity. No storage key exists.

Deleting it

Remove any audit from your history on the dashboard; deleting a private audit erases its result. Delete my account, at the bottom of the dashboard, erases your profile, history, tokens, connected apps and every private audit at once. Justify is open source: read exactly what it does.
